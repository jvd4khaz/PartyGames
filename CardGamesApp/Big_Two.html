Big Two
Quick Reference
Goal: Get rid of all cards first. Others score penalty points for cards left.
Start: Player with the weakest card (usually the Green 3) leads the first trick.
On turn: Play a stronger combo with the same card count, or Pass.
Trick ends: When everyone else passes after a play. Last player to play leads next.
Players
3-4 players
Deck
Standard 52-card deck (basic rules)
Difficulty
Medium
Play Time
20-40 minutes
Overview

Big Two is a traditional climbing card game from East Asia. Players must play a combination that beats the previous combination. The 2 is the strongest rank. If a player cannot (or does not want to) beat the current play, they pass.

Components (as referenced)
  • 52 basic cards: 2-10, J, Q, K, A in four colors each
  • Optional: 2 dragon cards, 4 reference cards, 3 additional cards (not used in basic rules)
Game Setup (Basic Rules)
  1. Each player receives 1 reference card.
  2. Shuffle the 52 basic cards and deal:
    • 3 players: 17 cards each
    • 4 players: 13 cards each
    If any cards remain (3-player games or variant rules), set them aside face down without looking.
  3. Players hold their cards hidden. The game begins.
Card Strength
Rank order (weak → strong):
3 < 4 < … < 9 < 10 < J < Q < K < A < 2
If ranks tie, compare color (weak → strong):
Green < Yellow < Red < Purple

Strongest single card: Purple 2. Weakest single card: Green 3.

Objective & Scoring

Big Two is played over several rounds. The first player to play all their cards wins the round and gains points equal to the total penalty points of the other players.

Penalty Points (Basic Rules)
Player Count Remaining Cards Penalty Points
4 players 1-8 cards cards × 1
4 players 9-12 cards cards × 2
3 players 1-11 cards cards × 1
3 players 12 cards cards × 2
3 players 13 cards cards × 3
3 players 17 cards cards × 3

Game end: If any player reaches -100 points or below, the game ends. Highest score wins.

Gameplay (Tricks)
  1. Lead: Player with the weakest card starts the first trick by playing a legal combination (face up).
  2. Follow: Next players (counterclockwise) must play a stronger combination with the same number of cards, or Pass.
  3. Passing: A player who passed may play again when their turn comes around.
  4. Trick ends: If all other players pass consecutively after a play, the trick ends. Played cards are set aside.
  5. Next trick: The last player to play leads the next trick with any legal combination.
Note: Players should not hide how many cards remain in their hand. Anyone may ask at any time.
Card Combinations

Cards are played in combinations of 1, 2, 3, or 5. Combinations are compared only against other combinations with the same number of cards.

Valid Combinations
# Cards Name Condition
1 Single Any 1 card
2 Pair 2 cards of the same rank
3 Triple 3 cards of the same rank
5 Straight 5 consecutive ranks
5 Flush 5 cards of the same color
5 Full House Triple + Pair
5 4 card + single 4 of a kind + any 1 card
5 Straight Flush Straight + Flush
5-card combo strength (weak → strong):
Straight < Flush < Full House < 4 card + single < Straight Flush

How comparisons work: Compare the strongest card ranks first (then second/third if needed). If ranks fully tie, compare the strongest card’s color.

Special straight rule: A can only be the beginning or end of a straight. Only two A-straights exist: A-2-3-4-5 and 10-J-Q-K-A.
Advanced Rules (Optional Levels)
  • Level 1 – Strong but Risky 2: Flashy Finish (last play is Single 2 doubles others’ penalties). Excessive Patience (any player holding a 2 doubles their penalty once).
  • Level 2 – Dragons: Add dragon cards. As singles they are strongest. In combos, they can act as any rank/color. Follow Level 1 scoring doubling rules.
  • Level 3 – Bombs: Treat “4 card + single” and “Straight Flush” as bombs. They can be played over non-5-card tricks and only beaten by stronger bombs. Also trigger doubling in scoring.
  • Level 4 – Equal Start: Low hand score can request reshuffle (count only J/Q/K/A/2 with 1/2/3/4/5 points respectively; if ≤3, reshuffle).
  • Level 5 – Irregular Dragons: Dragons can be played anytime as singles, but any card can beat dragons. Other rules same as Level 4.

When using advanced rules, score swings are larger. Recommended game end is -200 points.
